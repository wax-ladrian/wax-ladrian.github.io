Hello all,

Today professor Francis Halzen, of the University of Wisconsin – Madison and from the Wisconsin IceCube Particle Astrophysics Center was awarded the 2026 Physics Nobel Prize!

[IceCube](https://icecube.wisc.edu/science/icecube/ "https://icecube.wisc.edu/science/icecube/"), for those who do not know about it, is an experiment located in the geographic South Pole, where they use the geological ice as a target for astrophysical neutrinos to interact and then be detected through those interactions. This experiment was the first reliable instance of doing extensive astrophysical research using messengers that were not light, and was a turning point in the field of Astronomy, literally opening new windows to the universe. The field of “multimessenger astrophysics” is a very young field and it is the field I am starting in my career, so it is exciting to see this achievement by professor Halzen, who not only had the original idea of using the Arctic ice as a detector, but also made the right connections and recruited the right people to get this idea into reality. 

Many months ago, I showed professor Halzen around my lab and I was showing him some of our fragile camera modules. As I handed him one, my advisor entered the lab and he said (paraphrased) “Oris, are you letting a theorist hold our fragile equipment?” he was, of course, kidding, and he was the one who took the photo bellow! But, just in case he was not fully kidding, I can now say “Well, I was letting a future Nobel Laureate hold our equipment, for luck!”
