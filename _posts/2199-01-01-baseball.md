# The game

Baseball is not a game of Tag, contrary to what I originally thought. The Tag aspect of it has only to do on making so that your opponents don't score, while in Tag per see, tagging is the thing that makes one get points. Although this is arbitrary, one can argue that in a game of Tag, you can define an individual getting a point based on not being tagged for some amount of time or until some condition is met. Anyway, baseball is better described as a game where people get points to their team if they go from A to A (closed loop) without being afflicted by the "out" condition.

## The basics

Baseball is a game where $N_T > 1$ teams play against each other and whichever team ends with more points win the game. Note that so far I have only observed the case of $N_T = 2$, so I will be using this value for the remaining of the document. I will get back to this in the end but I believe that this game is almost easily generalizable to any number of teams.

There are different stages of the game, but the actual play happens on what I will call "Running attempts ($RAs$)." Understanding that unit of the game is extremely relevant so I will jump to explaining that now and get back to the basics afterward.

## The structure of an $RA$

On a $RA$, one team is the one taking the running attempt, we will call them the running team, while the other team (remember, this is $N_T = 2$ case) is playing as the pitching team. **Only the runner team can score in this $RA$, and the $RA$ ends when a "run" is done or when an "out" condition is reached, whichever happens first.**

The running team elects one member as the runner (the "how" of that decision, or the limitations of such decision, are a mystery to me, tho as far as I understand they are not allowed to just use the same person over and over again).

The pitching team elects one member to be the pitcher, and one member to be the catcher (as well, unclear how the elect decision is done). The pitcher positions themselves at the pitching spot, some distance from the catcher. The catcher and the runner position themselves at the Home, which is one of $N_b$ special places which I will call the bases (so far I have only seen $N_b = 4$ : Home, $1^\text{st}$ base, $2^\text{nd}$ base and $3^\text{rd}$ base). The pitcher will then throw the ball. The runner is positioned such that they can hit the ball with their bat if the ball flies to the catcher. Once the pitcher throws the ball, one out of 4 things can happen:
### A "strike"

A "strike" happens when the runners swing the bat past a certain swinging angle and does not hit the ball thrown to the catcher. Usually the catcher catches the ball.

Three strikes means an "out" and the $RA$ ends.
### A "ball"

A "ball" when a swing by the runner is not done, and when the ball flies in a trajectory that does not pass through what is called the Box, a 2D rectangular window plane, somewhere in between the pitching spot and Home. Note, the "ball" condition can happen at the same time as a "run" condition, more on that on the "run" section.
### A "run"

The game really only happens during a "run", everything else in baseball is a complex system of checks and rules that are used to decide whether a run happens or not. 

A "run" happens when the ball is "in air". The "in air" conditions can take many forms:

- The ball was thrown to the catcher and the runner hit it to a direction not off the perimeter of the field side-lines.
- The ball was thrown to some other player of the pitcher's team, usually done in order to tag other players in from the runner's team who are in basis and might attempt to advance. Note that this means this is a "ball" condition too.

During the "run" condition, the players in the runner's team are trying to advance in bases. One can only go to the next base if they come from the base before it. To leave Home, the runner must have hit the ball. The order is: Home -> $1^\text{st}$ base -> $2^\text{nd}$ base -> ... -> $(N_b-1)^\text{th}$ base -> Home. Whenever a player reaches Home, completing the loop, their team gets a point. Any players of the runner's team who are in bases may attempt to advance during the "run" condition. In between bases, a player of the runner's team is considered "vulnerable." 

If the ball was hit by the runner, and it is caught by someone in the pitching team before it touches the ground, that is an automatic "out". In this context, any "vulnerable" runner team player is automatically out of the base circuit, and the runner is also out regardless of progress made or vulnerability status, and that ends the $RA$. If points were made before the catch of the ball... I am not sure whether those are nullified or not. If however, it hits the ground before anyone in the pitching team catches the ball, then the "game of tag" starts.

#### The game of tag

Once the ball touches the ground, that $RA$ will end once no "vulnerable" players in the running team are in play anymore (in here I am overlooking the instantaneous instances of players going out and in of the "vulnerability" status in virtue of them touching a base on their way of advancing). A "vulnerable" player becomes out of play when they're tagged or when they reach Home and complete the circuit, scoring a point to the running team and exiting play. A running team player is not "vulnerable" and not out of play when they're in a numbered base and have reached such base without being tagged. Also, the runner is not "vulnerable" if they have reached $1^\text{st}$ base and have not yet touched $2^\text{nd}$ base.

To be tagged, a running team player must be in a "vulnerable" state (in between bases) and be touched by a pitching team player who possesses the ball. The running team player is also considered tagged if they touch a base that has a pitching team player with the ball in their possession touching that base.

Note that for a running team player in play, only three outcomes can come out of a run:
- Reaching back to Home
- Stopping safely in a base
- Being tagged and out of play

There is then the condition of "Home Run"

#### Home Run

A "Home Run" happens when the runner hits the ball and the ball goes in the allowed direction past the baseball field. This means that the game of tag does start but no tag can happen (it will start because the ball will be "in air" until it hits the ground and since no player of the pitching team can reach it, it will hit the ground before it is caught). Since no tag can happen, the only way the $RA$ ends is with the runner and all the players in the running team who were in play, in bases, reaching Home (therefore acquiring a point). The running team can get at most $N_b$ points in this "Home Run" (if there is one player in each of the $N_b$ bases, including here the runner at the Home base).

#### A "walk"

If "ball" happens 4 times, that trigger a "walk," which means every running team player gets to move up 1 base for free, including the runner who then goes to $1^\text{st}$ base. The $RA$ ends after the "walk."

## Game structure

Each team gets to play consecutive $RAs$ until 3 "Outs" have happened, at which point, any players in bases are out of play and the next team gets to start their consecutive $RAs$ . Once 3 outs per team have happened, that is called a Inning ($Is$). A game of baseball goes on until both these following conditions are true upon the end of an Inning:
- There has been at least 8 Innings
- The game is not tied

Note that the second condition guarantees no game ever ends in a tie, and the first condition guarantees there are at least 8 innings in a game.
